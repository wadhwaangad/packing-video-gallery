A packing simulator that generates new trajectories on demand.

A person packs a suitcase in a five-room apartment, driven either by a sampled case (a task, a persona and a private schedule of mistakes) or by instructions in plain language. Every request is grounded into a typed action, refused with a reason if it cannot be, and executed in a contact-only MuJoCo simulation with anatomical hands. That covers the case's zip, combination lock, strap buckle, telescopic handle and spinner wheels, and folding a towel. Each run is rendered and logged as training data.

Try it in your browser: the playground runs the simulated user live (the same Python code, through Pyodide, no GPU needed). You type as the assistant while they pack. It also has replays of every event type, each with and without an assistant, and rendered physics episodes with the map and conversation synced to the video.

How the simulation works

The design follows the environment construction of interactive-assistant benchmarks: an executable task rather than a recorded demonstration, a typed action space with a grounder, goal predicates instead of a reference route, and progress-conditioned events. Here it is specialised to packing.

1 · RequestA sampled case (task, persona, private event schedule), or instructions: sentences parsed into requests, or a JSON plan of actions.
2 · GroundEach request becomes a typed action: words resolved to things in the scene, values parsed and checked against the part they act on. Otherwise it is rejected with a code and a reason.
3 · PackSimSymbolic state, preconditions, effects and goal predicates. A refused action returns feedback and changes nothing.
4 · ExecutorGrounds each accepted action in MuJoCo with contact-only skills, measures the physical outcome, then commits it. Any action can be rewound.
5 · Record25 Hz poses, the action log with labels and frame ranges, physical checks, hardware readings and goals. Blender Cycles renders head, room and close-up cameras.

State

Where the person stands and what they face; what they hold; each item's location (home station, hands, or the case) and, in the case, its measured centre, turn, height and what it rests on; the bottle's orientation; the lid; how far the zip is open; whether the zip pulls are docked in the lock and what the three dials show; the trolley handle's stop; the strap buckle; whether the case lies on the bed or stands on the floor, and where; the loose towel (flat or folded) when the scene has one; and the current packing list. Physical state lives in MuJoCo and is cross-checked against the symbolic state at the end of every run: every item, the zip, the dials, the pulls, the handle stop and the buckle.

Goal predicates

Success is a set of predicates, not a route, so any valid order passes: packed(x) for every listed item, no_extra_items, bottle_upright, heavy_on_floor(x), nothing_on(camera), lid_closed, zipped, case_upright and, for tasks that ask for it, locked.

Action space

The action space is a closed catalogue of fully specified actions, such as pick_up(trousers), place_on(tshirts, trousers), zip_to(50), enter(hall) or turn_dial(dial_2, 7). Every action the simulator runs, from a sampled case or from an instruction, is one of these ids, and each record logs it. The catalogue holds what a person can really do with these things and what the simulation carries out. Anything real that is not yet reliable in simulation is left out, with the reason. The catalogue is built to leave no room for a malformed action:

  1. No free values. Every argument comes from a finite list fixed by the scene: rooms, stations, items, the five named spots in the case, another packed item and a side of it, dial digits, handle stops, floor spots. A quantity a person really varies is taken in steps a person would use: the zip runs to 0, 25, 50, 75 or 100 % closed, either way. There are no coordinates, angles, durations or free text.
  2. No optional arguments or defaults. An id is the whole action. enter(room) always ends at the same point of that room for a given starting place.
  3. One id, one skill, one effect. No verb covers two parts (open_lid, zip_to), and no effect has two ids: a lock code is set dial by dial, and there is no separate set-code action.
  4. Real and reliable. These real actions are left out for now: .
  5. The legal actions are listed. At any moment the simulator lists the ids whose preconditions hold now: hands free, lid open, same room or in view for go_to, a neighbouring room for enter. An agent that chooses from that list cannot make a grounding or precondition error. Only the physical outcome can still fail, and that is measured.

Grounding: from words to an action, or a reason why not

Grounding happens when a request is reached, against the state at that moment, so it or the pile on the dresser resolve against what is there then. Reference resolution works in four steps:

  1. A pronoun names the item in hand, else the last item mentioned.
  2. An exact label or phrase of an allowed kind wins.
  3. Otherwise every content word must belong to a candidate's vocabulary: its nouns, modifiers and where it is now. The last word must be one of its nouns.
  4. One match resolves the reference. None gives unknown_ref. Several give ambiguous_ref with the candidates listed, unless exactly one of them has the most modifiers matched.

Values are parsed into their types and checked against the part's own range: the lid's allowed openings, the handle's stops, a dial's digits. The grounded action must then be a catalogue action, before anything moves. Only deterministic rewrites are made: a lock code becomes one turn_dial per dial, clicks become the digit they land on, "go to the kitchen" becomes enter(kitchen), and a pack request with no spot takes the planner's named spot. "beside the trousers" with no side takes the first side (in a fixed order) where it fits. Ambiguous words are still resolved, or returned with their candidates, as above. Anything else is rejected as not_in_action_space with the reason: a zip position between the steps, a spot in centimetres, an item turned by an angle, or an action in the excluded list. Then the symbolic preconditions run. If the target is out of reach, a go_to is inserted first; nothing else is added silently.

Moving between rooms

The person knows where everything is, but one go_to only takes them to something in the room they are in, or in view from where they stand. In view means an unobstructed line from eye height (1.55 m) to the target, past the walls, door frames and furniture. Anything else is reached room by room:

  1. enter(room) for each room on the way, walking through the doorway (or across the open-plan boundary) to the first point of the walking graph inside it.
  2. After each room, the target is checked again; the steps stop as soon as it is in its room or in view.
  3. look_at(item) for an item that has to be found, with a ray cast in the physics scene that records whether it was really in sight.
  4. The final go_to.

Each step is logged as its own action, with its own time span and frames, and names the request it came from (parent). The same run can be used at the level of the request or of the steps. With --no-reach nothing is inserted or expanded, so an out-of-view go_to is refused with the reason: a strict mode for evaluating an agent that has to plan its own route.

Instructions and parsers

python packing/generate.py RUN --say "pack the t-shirts at the handle end; close the lid; zip it; lock it"
python packing/generate.py RUN --plan plan.json          # [{"verb": "place", "args": {...}}, ...] or sentences
python packing/generate.py RUN --say "..." --parser gemini   # needs GEMINI_API_KEY (GEMINI_MODEL optional)

Two parsers produce the same request format. The rule parser covers every verb, compound sentences ("and then") and named values ("halfway", "the top stop", "3-7-2"). The Gemini parser fills a fixed JSON schema of verbs and argument slots. It is told to copy the user's own words for every reference and never to choose between objects, so the same deterministic resolver decides what the words mean, and ambiguity is caught the same way. A step that fits no verb comes back as unknown_verb.

The suitcase's own action space

Every part and control of the scanned case is a separate simulated body, so each operation changes the state by contact:

  • The zip sliders run on slide joints.
  • The pulls swing up on hinges into the lock's slots.
  • The dials turn on hinges with ten detents, and buttons move on springs.
  • The telescopic handle slides on a latch that is free only while its button is held in.
  • The buckle slides out once both side tabs are squeezed.
  • The four spinner wheels are spheres on ball joints.

Values a part cannot take are rejected, with the part's own reason.

Items and cloth

Items are operated through pick_up, place and put_back. Their family decides the grasp and how they are set down: a two-hand side squeeze for piles and boxes, a one-hand power grip for the bottle, which stands upright or lies down. The loose beach towel is a simulated flexible sheet (a MuJoCo flex grid with in-plane and bending elasticity) on the kitchen counter, and fold folds it in half by contact. It is added to the scene only for cases that use it, because it multiplies the cost of the physics.

    Progress-conditioned events

    An event has a semantic trigger (for example "about to fetch the sweatshirts" or "after three items are packed"), targets, the actions it injects, and a completion condition. While active it owns the user's action queue, then hands control back. With self-recovery on, the user corrects the mistake: puts the wrong pile back, remembers the forgotten item before closing, reopens the lid. With it off, the error stays in the world and shows up as a failed goal. Questions and plan changes use separate, non-error channels.

    Sampled trajectories

    Eight cases sampled with generate.py --seed N (the last with --task work_trip) and run in physics with the published code, cross-room walks taken room by room. A trajectory that fails any check is rejected, not kept as data. Four of the eight pass every check. Two fail reopening the lid after closing it too early: the hand hooks the edge but the lid does not lift (the same failure on the code before room-by-room navigation). One fails closing the lid (6° short), and one fails when a shirt does not land where it was placed. Failed actions are first rewound and retried with small variations (see limitations).     What is simulated, and the limitations
    • MuJoCo at 1 kHz with gravity. Two anatomical MyoHand hands (23 joints each) with torque-limited finger servos on force-limited wrist carriages. Nothing is welded to a hand: items, zip sliders, pulls, dials, buttons, the buckle and the handle move only through contact.
    • Fingertip and pinch targets come from the hand model itself. The fingertip is the end of the distal collision capsule. The pinch shapes are solved so the thumb and index capsules meet, and their midpoint and axis are measured on the model before each use.
    • Idealised mechanisms, each stated where it applies:
      • Zip: the lid is held shut by narrowing its hinge range once more than 5% of the zip is closed, the lid is down, and the closure check passes. The sliders cover the front side of the track; they park at its front corners when open.
      • Latches: the lock's pull latch, the handle's stop latch and the buckle's click are narrowed joint ranges. They are released only by the measured button travel or squeeze force.
      • Dial detents: a periodic spring torque.
      • Hinge: the case's fabric hinge is a revolute joint at mid-depth. The hinge-side walls meet at its axis, so neither half sweeps into the other's packing space.
    • The lid can only be closed or fully open. On a real case it does not stay part-way, so other values are rejected with that reason rather than faked.
    • Rolling: the case rolls alongside the person on four ball-jointed casters, with the near hand on the extended handle. The hand corrects the case's lean from its measured tilt. It can be rolled between the foot of the bed and the bedroom door.
    • Cloth: only the loose beach towel is cloth (a 5 × 7 vertex flex sheet, rendered as a smooth subdivided surface). Folding it in half works by sliding flat hands under its hanging edge. Folding in thirds and unfolding are rejected: the edge they need lies flat, and the coarse sheet cannot be bunched into a pinch. The scanned garment piles are compliant rigid bodies, so they cannot be folded.
    • Not simulated, and rejected with a reason: changing the lock's combination (it needs a pen in the reset hole); carrying the case by its side handle; laying the standing case back down.
    • The body is kinematic, solved around the simulated forearms with fixed segment lengths: no balance, foot forces or whole-body collision. Pelvis clearance is checked geometrically.
    • If an action fails physically, the generator rewinds physics, recordings and state to just before it and retries with a small variation, up to three times. Retries are logged; a run that still fails is rejected.
    • The scripted user is not a learned human model. Masses, friction and strengths are assumed values; nothing is calibrated sim-to-real.
