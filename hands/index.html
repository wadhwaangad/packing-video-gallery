What changed?

  • Appearance: a continuous skin surface, a shaped palm and thumb base, separate fingers, and fingernails, deformed by the same recorded anatomical joints.
  • Mechanics: separate forearm rotation, wrist bending, thumb joints, and finger joints. Finite actuator strength, anatomical joint limits, inter-finger collisions, and frictional tool contact.
  • Turning: short forearm strokes, finger opening, clearance, reset, and regrasp. Tool motion comes from contact forces; it is not animated to follow the hand.

What this does—and does not—establish

  • The lift test must maintain contact and clearance under gravity, then finish with zero hand–tool contacts. The turn test must advance the tool through four bounded strokes, with contact-free resets.
  • The joints use torque-limited position servos. The source anatomy has muscle actuators, but this experiment does not use a muscle controller.
  • The soft contact solver permits small penetrations and small joint-limit excursions; the measurements are reported below. The skin is a visual surface over rigid contact shapes, not simulated deformable tissue.
  • These are nominal-condition tests, not proof of human realism or a closed sim-to-real gap. Whole-arm dynamics and full-body balance remain to be validated.
