Talk to a simulated person while they pack.

You play the assistant. The person has a packing list, a persona and a hidden schedule of slips, questions and requests. Say something to stop a mistake before it happens, answer their questions, or stay quiet and see whether they catch it themselves. The simulator runs in your browser: the same Python code as the generator, loaded with Pyodide, with no GPU or physics needed.

    About this page

    Play live samples a case the same way the generator does (episodes.sample_case): a task, a persona, and a schedule of events that is kept only if a silent run and an oracle run both work. The user replans from its own beliefs after every action, and the harness takes over its action queue when an error fires. What you type goes through the same reader as any assistant (dialogue.interpret), so the person may take your advice, ignore it (personas differ in compliance and gullibility), or ask what you mean. Advice takes effect between actions.

    The clock is simulated time: each action takes the median duration measured on the physics runs, and speech takes time too. The map draws the walking graph's route between stand points. The physics episodes follow the real recorded motion in their videos.

    Turn on show what the assistant can't see to see the schedule, step labels (plan, harness, guidance), commit points, how your messages were read, and the user's beliefs. The outcomes at the end are the same ones the generator records: prevented, corrected, self-recovered, unresolved; answered right or wrong; reminded or missed.
